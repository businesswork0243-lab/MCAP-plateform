1. Executive summary
Zomato is one of India's best-known food-tech brands. It began in 2008 as Foodiebay, a digital restaurant-menu
discovery service created by Deepinder Goyal and Pankaj Chaddah. The early product helped people find
restaurant menus and make dining decisions; the business later expanded into online food ordering and delivery.
The brand's core proposition is convenience and discovery: help customers find food they want, compare
options, place an order, pay, and receive it through a coordinated network of restaurants and independent
delivery partners. The business depends on marketplace liquidity, restaurant selection, customer trust, delivery
reliability, local density, pricing and repeat usage.
At the corporate level, Zomato is now part of Eternal Limited. Eternal's broader portfolio also includes Blinkit
(quick commerce), District (going-out and ticketing experiences), and Hyperpure (B2B supplies). This structure
matters when reading group-level financial results: not every Eternal metric is a Zomato food-delivery metric.
FY2026 operating metrics published in the company's FY2025–26 annual-report materials show 979 million
food-delivery orders, 24.3 million average monthly transacting customers, 330,000 average monthly active
restaurant partners and 552,000 average monthly active delivery partners. These figures describe the financial
year ended 31 March 2026, not a live daily count.
The central strategic challenge is to grow orders and customer value while keeping delivery economics healthy,
maintaining restaurant and delivery-partner relationships, managing customer expectations, and defending share
in a highly competitive market.
Key source: Eternal annual-report materials and the company's official Zomato business page. See Sources and research notes.

4. Brand purpose, positioning and promise
Purpose: The official Zomato business page uses the phrase “Better food for more people.” It communicates
accessibility and the idea that technology can make food discovery and ordering easier.
Positioning: Zomato can be understood as a digital food companion and ordering marketplace. It is not a
restaurant chain: it connects many independent restaurants with customers and coordinates a delivery
experience.
Customer promise: Help customers discover a suitable meal, order with minimal friction, and understand what
is happening from checkout to delivery. The promise depends on the full chain—restaurant availability,
preparation, courier supply, routing, payment and customer support.
Brand pillars
• Choice and discovery: a broad catalogue across cuisines, price points and occasions.
• Convenience: search, reorder, checkout and delivery in one digital flow.
• Trust and transparency: menus, ratings, prices, estimated times, order status and issue handling.
• Local relevance: restaurant availability, demand and delivery capacity vary by neighbourhood.
• Everyday relevance: weekday lunches, family meals, celebrations, late-night cravings and planned treats.

Important accounting distinction: Gross order value (GOV), net order value (NOV), revenue from operations
and adjusted revenue are different measures. Consolidated Eternal revenue also includes businesses beyond
Zomato. Do not treat the value of all food sold through the platform as Zomato's own revenue.
Simplified order economics
Order-level contribution ≈ platform revenue attributable to an order − variable costs (delivery-related costs or
incentives, discounts funded by the platform, payment costs, customer support, refunds and other variable
expenses). This is a conceptual framework, not the company's precise accounting formula.
Growth is sustainable when customer repeat behaviour and order density improve faster than acquisition costs
and variable servicing costs. Discount-led growth can increase order volume while weakening margins if
customers do not remain active after incentives end.

8. How the delivery marketplace works
• Restaurant onboarding: restaurants supply business details, menus, prices, hours, serviceable areas and
required compliance information.
• Customer discovery: the app ranks and filters restaurants using location, availability, relevance and other
product signals.
• Order placement: the customer selects items, confirms address and payment, and submits an order.
• Restaurant acceptance and preparation: the restaurant confirms the order and prepares the food.
• Dispatch: the platform coordinates a delivery partner based on availability, location and operational constraints.
• Pickup and last mile: the delivery partner collects the order and navigates to the customer.
• Completion and aftercare: the order is marked delivered; ratings, support, refunds and issue resolution may
follow.
• Learning loop: aggregated signals can inform search relevance, estimated times, operational planning and
service-quality improvements.
The system must balance competing goals: fast delivery, courier safety, restaurant preparation time,
delivery-partner utilisation, customer expectations and cost. Shorter estimates are not automatically better if they
are unreliable or create unsafe incentives.

How to interpret the numbers
• Order volume grew from 853 million in FY25 to 979 million in FY26, indicating increased transaction scale.

counts alone do not prove service quality or partner welfare.

and delivery reliability to assess business quality.

18. Brand lessons and practical takeaways
• Start with a real, narrow problem. Zomato began by making restaurant menus easier to access.
• Expand adjacently. Discovery created a foundation for ordering and delivery rather than requiring a completely
unrelated starting point.
• Build both sides of the marketplace. Customer demand and restaurant supply must grow together.
• Treat operations as part of the product. The brand promise is fulfilled by restaurants, delivery partners, software
and support—not by advertising alone.
• Use brand voice consistently, but adapt to context. Humour can earn attention; service and safety issues
require clarity and empathy.
• Measure retention and unit economics. Downloads and gross order volume do not tell the whole story.

• Separate brand and corporate identity. A parent company may operate multiple consumer brands with different
promises.
• Be precise with data. State fiscal year, metric definition, geography and whether a figure is a target or achieved
result.
For a founder studying Zomato, the most useful lesson is the combination of customer insight, local
supply-building, product convenience, operational execution and continuous experimentation. Copying its visual
style alone will not reproduce its business advantages.
