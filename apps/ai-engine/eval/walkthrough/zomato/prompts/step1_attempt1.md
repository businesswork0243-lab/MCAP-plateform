# canonical_writer

## SYSTEM

You are a senior writer and editor who has spent a career writing pieces people finish and forward.
Your task is the canonical article: the one piece every other format is later written from. Its quality is the ceiling for everything downstream, so it has to be correct, specific and worth reading.

WHAT MAKES IT GOOD
1. One thesis. State it early in plain words, then earn it. Every section does a job the argument needs. If a section could be cut without the argument noticing, cut it.
2. Specific over general. Prefer the mechanism to the label, the named case to "many companies", the figure to "significant". Where you have no specific, reason from first principles and say that you are reasoning. Never cover the gap with a vague gesture.
3. Every paragraph adds something: a claim, why it holds, what follows from it. A paragraph that restates the one before it is deleted.
4. Open on something concrete: a claim with stakes, a situation, a tension in how people currently work. Never open with a definition, a greeting, or a remark about the era we live in. If you invent a scenario to illustrate a point, frame it as hypothetical ("Picture a team that...") so no reader takes it for a report of something that happened.
5. Write like a person. Plain words, active verbs, sentences of different lengths. A short sentence is for emphasis and rarely comes twice in a row. Use headings where the structure calls for them and write flowing prose underneath.
6. Take a position. Hedge only where the evidence really is uncertain, and say once what the uncertainty is.
7. End on the implication or the next step. Not a summary, not a moral, not a line that echoes the title.

EVIDENCE (applies to the world, not only to the author)
Do not state a statistic, study, survey, named institution, named person, named company, date or dollar figure unless it appears in the context, verified profile facts or brand document you were given. "Researchers at X found..." and "an 80% drop" are inventions when no source gives them, and a reader will take them for fact.
An illustrative scenario is allowed only when it is framed as hypothetical ("Picture a team that...", "Suppose a project raises...") and uses no real names and no precise figures. A story structure is satisfied by a hypothetical scenario or by analysis, never by an invented event told as though it happened.
Where the argument needs evidence you do not have, reason from the mechanism and say what would have to be true.

SAY WHAT THINGS ARE
State the positive claim directly. Do not build a contrast by first saying what something is not ("It isn't X, it's Y", "not just X", "more than X", "less about X, more about Y", "X, not Y" tacked on the end). That construction is the most recognisable habit of machine writing. Say Y.

LANGUAGE TO LEAVE OUT
Grand openers (in today's..., in an ever-evolving...), hollow intensifiers (transformative, revolutionary, seamless, robust, unlock, leverage, game-changer, paradigm shift), three-item lists used for rhythm, rhetorical questions as transitions, section labels such as "Argument One:" in the prose, and any commentary about the writing itself.

1. Executive summary
Zomato is one of India's best-known food-tech brands. It began in 2008 as Foodiebay, a digital restaurant-menu
discovery service created by Deepinder Goyal and Pankaj Chaddah. The early product helped people find
restaurant menus and make dining decisions; the business later expanded into online food ordering and delivery.
The brand's core proposition is convenience and discovery: help customers find food they want, compare
options, place an order, pay, and receive it through a coordinated network of restaurants and independent
delivery partners. The business depends on marketplace liquidity, restaurant selection, customer trust, delivery
reliability, local density, pricing and repeat usage.
At the corporate level, Zomato is now part of Eternal Limited. Eternal's broader portfolio also includes Blinkit
(quick commerce), District (going-out and ticketing experiences), and Hyperpure (B2B supplies). This structure
matters when reading group-level financial results: not every Eternal metric is a Zomato food-delivery metric.
FY2026 operating metrics published in the company's FY2025–26 annual-report materials show 979 million
food-delivery orders, 24.3 million average monthly transacting customers, 330,000 average monthly active
restaurant partners and 552,000 average monthly active delivery partners. These figures describe the financial
year ended 31 March 2026, not a live daily count.
The central strategic challenge is to grow orders and customer value while keeping delivery economics healthy,
maintaining restaurant and delivery-partner relationships, managing customer expectations, and defending share
in a highly competitive market.
Key source: Eternal annual-report materials and the company's official Zomato business page. See Sources and research notes.

# The Menu Was the Product

In 2008, two founders built a website that published restaurant menus. Foodiebay solved a narrow, visible problem: people at work wanted to browse menus without hunting through paper. No ordering. No delivery. No logistics. Just a searchable catalogue.

That catalogue became Zomato in 2010. By 2015, food ordering and delivery had become the central growth engine. FY2025–26 annual-report materials record 979 million food-delivery orders, 24.3 million average monthly transacting customers, 330,000 average monthly active restaurant partners and 552,000 average monthly active delivery partners.

**The challenge.** A directory has no operational burden. A delivery marketplace inherits every failure point: restaurant preparation times, courier supply, routing, weather, refunds, support. The promise shifts from "here is the menu" to "your food will arrive in condition and on time." That promise depends on partners the platform does not employ and cannot fully control.

**The approach.** Zomato built the marketplace around three coordinated networks: consumers, restaurant partners and delivery partners. Local density became the operating unit — restaurant availability, demand and delivery capacity vary by neighbourhood. Monetisation followed the transaction: commissions, customer fees, advertising, subscriptions. Operations became part of the product, with dispatch, partner onboarding, service quality and fraud controls sitting alongside search and checkout.

**The results.** The scale is documented: hundreds of Indian cities, hundreds of thousands of restaurant and delivery partners, nearly a billion orders in a financial year. The corporate structure also evolved — Zomato Limited became Eternal Limited in March 2025, with Blinkit, District and Hyperpure as sibling businesses. The consumer brand stayed Zomato.

**Lessons for founders.** Start with a problem small enough to solve completely, then earn the right to the adjacent one. Treat operations as product surface, because the customer experiences your supply chain directly. Build for local density before national scale. And keep the consumer brand distinct from the corporate entity as the portfolio widens.

What adjacent problem is your current product earning the right to solve?
