A pension fund settles a bond trade on a public chain. The authority to approve that settlement lives somewhere else entirely.

That gap is where institutional blockchain programmes quietly stall.

The thesis: institutional authority and the execution network are two separate systems. Institutions that treat them as one will build governance they cannot audit and execution they cannot move.

Three reasons this matters.

Durability. Execution networks come and go. Chains fork, merge, deprecate, get replaced. An institution's authority to act — who may bind it, under what conditions, with what revocation rights — must outlast any single network.

Auditability. The Verifiability Rule holds that a system's trustworthiness depends on whether its claims can be independently checked. Authority recorded inside a smart contract is checkable only by reading that chain.

Operational reality. Treasury, custody, and settlement teams already run on multi-network assumptions. When authority is network-specific, every new chain multiplies approval workflows, key management, and reconciliation.

Consider a bank issuing a payment credential valid across three chains. Revocation happens once, in the authority layer. The chains execute; they do not adjudicate.
